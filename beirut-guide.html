The Beirut Guide · For Families Joining Eastwood

A city worth knowing.

We live here. Here is what matters.

A short, practical guide to settling in Beirut, written for the families who join us. Where to live, how the city works, and how to make it yours.

The Short Version

Beirut, before
you arrive.

Where you'll live
The Metn hills

Families settle around Mansourieh, Ain Saadeh, Rabieh and Beit Mery — greener and cooler than the coast, with central Beirut 20–40 minutes away.

Getting around
You'll want a car

There's no real public transit in the Metn. Uber and Bolt fill the gaps, and the local servees shared taxis soon become second nature.

Money & power
USD and a generator

Rent and larger costs run in US dollars; a private generator subscription is standard, and your landlord introduces you to the local supplier.

Language
Trilingual, and easy

Arabic, French and English move fluidly through the same conversation. English will carry you everywhere from day one.

Before you land
Insurance & residency

Secure comprehensive international health cover before arrival, and start residency through your employer or sponsor ahead of the move.

The feeling
A city worth knowing

World-class food, a Mediterranean coastline, and ski slopes an hour away. Beirut rewards the curious.

Where Families Live

Neighbourhoods
near campus.

Most Eastwood families settle in the hill towns of the Metn, minutes from campus.

01
Mansourieh

The campus town itself. Residential streets are established and walkable, daily errands are close at hand, and the school gate is a short walk or the briefest of drives. For families who want to eliminate the morning run entirely, this is the obvious choice.

02
Ain Saadeh and Mtayleb

Two quiet hillside neighbourhoods sitting just above campus, popular with families who want a little more space and greenery without meaningfully adding distance to the school run. The atmosphere is residential and unhurried.

03
Rabieh

A gated, leafy enclave favoured by diplomatic and expat families. Well-maintained grounds, a calm day-to-day rhythm, and a strong international community make it a reliable first posting for families arriving with young children.

04
Beit Mery and Broumana

Higher up the mountain and noticeably cooler in summer. The commute is longer and more scenic, and the weekend culture of restaurants, walks, and village markets gives these neighbourhoods a distinct character. Families who settle here tend to appreciate the distance from city pace.

Settling In

Your first
month.

Residency and paperwork

Most expat families arrive on an employment or sponsorship visa and convert to a residency permit shortly after landing. Begin the process through your employer or sponsor before you travel. Documents typically required include passport copies, employment contract, and medical clearance certificates for all family members.

Banking and currency

Open a USD account at one of the established private banks on arrival. Day-to-day spending runs on a parallel cash economy: USD for larger transactions and rent, Lebanese pounds for small purchases and local services. Bring sufficient cash to cover your first few weeks while banking is arranged.

Healthcare

Private clinics and hospitals across the Metn are well equipped and accustomed to international families. Arrange comprehensive family health insurance before you arrive. The school can point you to the facilities most commonly used by Eastwood families, including specialist options in Mansourieh and Sin el Fil.

Connectivity and utilities

Mobile lines are available from Alfa and Touch at any major branch. Home internet is generally reliable and contracts are straightforward. A private generator subscription is standard practice across the Metn: your landlord will introduce you to the local supplier, and the monthly cost sits alongside your electricity budget.

Living Here

The rhythm
of the place.

What's on: Agenda Culturel →

The campus sits in the centre of Mansourieh, and most families from the surrounding hill towns drive. The run from Mansourieh, Ain Saadeh, and Rabieh is short enough to be unremarkable; from Beit Mery and Broumana it is longer but consistent. Eastwood also operates a managed bus service across the Metn, with route and catchment detail available on request.

Beyond the school gate, Beirut rewards the curious: world-class food, a Mediterranean coastline, and ski slopes an hour away. The cash-and-USD economy takes a week to understand and a month to feel easy in, and the brief generator switch-over as the public grid changes becomes background noise faster than most people expect.

What surprises new families most is how quickly the community closes around them. Eastwood is small enough that new arrivals are noticed, welcomed, and absorbed into school life almost from the first week.
