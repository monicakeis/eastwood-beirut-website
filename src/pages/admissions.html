How is placement decided from Grade 1?

We review the last three years of report cards and the Ministry-certified completion certificate, and the student sits a short internal evaluation.

My child is still learning English.

Students who need extra English take a placement assessment for our English as a Second Language programme.

My child is neurodivergent.

We ask for all assessments and documentation, and your child spends two trial days with us so we can observe before we decide together.

We are applying from outside Lebanon.

Students entering Grades 2 to 12 need transcripts certified by the Lebanese Embassy or Consulate in the country they are leaving.

Why we ask for full disclosure.

Please share any prior diagnosis or support for developmental, speech, learning, social-emotional or physical needs when you apply, so the right support is in place from the first day. If material information is withheld and later comes to light, the school may decline or withdraw enrolment, and fees already paid are not refunded.

Moving to
Lebanon.

We live here. This is the short version of what families ask us before they move, checked against how things stand in November 2026.

  1. The campus town

    Mansourieh

    Established residential streets, errands close at hand, and the school gate a short walk or drive away.

  2. Just above campus

    Ain Saadeh & Mtayleb

    Quiet hillside neighbourhoods with more space and greenery, for barely any extra time on the school run.

  3. A short drive

    Rabieh

    Leafy and calm, with a long-standing international community.

  4. Higher up the mountain

    Beit Mery & Broumana

    Cooler in summer, a longer and more scenic drive, and a weekend life of restaurants, walks and village markets.

Residency

Working in Lebanon takes a work permit from the Ministry of Labour and a residence permit from General Security, usually arranged by the employer. Families of a foreign worker with annual residency can be granted it on the same terms. Start before you travel.

Money

Everyday life runs largely in US dollars, and largely in cash. Accounts and cards in "fresh" dollars work normally; bring enough cash for your first weeks while you set one up.

Power

The state grid covers only a few hours a day, so homes run on a neighbourhood generator subscription, often with solar panels and batteries too. Ask any landlord what the building has before you sign.

Getting around

In the Metn you will want a car. Uber and Bolt fill the gaps, and the school runs a bus service across the Metn; ask us about routes.

Phone and internet

Two mobile operators, Alfa and Touch, both now rolling out 5G. Home internet contracts are straightforward.

Health

Arrange international family health cover before you arrive. Private hospitals and clinics in the Metn are used to international families, and we can tell you which ones our families use.

Before you decide

Read your own government's current travel advice for Lebanon, and ask us anything. We will give you a straight answer.
